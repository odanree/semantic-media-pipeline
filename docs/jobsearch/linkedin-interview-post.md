## PATH A: I GOT IT 🎉
### Post 1 — The Wait (publish first, same day as result)

Three weeks of first round interviews, and it came down to two candidates.

The final round was in the morning. After we wrapped, they told me the other candidate was coming in later that afternoon and a decision would follow shortly after. I spent the rest of the day in that specific kind of limbo that every job seeker knows — where there's nothing left to do but wait and replay every answer you gave.

The interview had two parts. The technical deep dive felt natural — I had months of real work behind me with honest stories about tradeoffs and failures. The live debugging exercise pushed me harder. There's a pressure that comes from debugging in front of an audience with a clock running that doesn't exist when it's just you and your terminal. I felt it. But I kept talking through my reasoning, traced the problem out loud, and found my way through.

I showed up prepared. I was honest when I was uncertain. I asked good questions. After that, it was out of my hands.

— More to come.

### Post 2 — The Result + Thank You (publish after, same day or next morning)

I got the job.

[1-2 sentences on what the role is and why it matters to you — fill in after the call]

Getting here wasn't just about the interview. Three weeks of final rounds and months of building in public taught me more than I expected. Five things that carried me through:

1. **Build something real.** When they asked about distributed systems, I didn't describe a concept — I described a pipeline I debugged at 2am. The work was the answer.

2. **Show your thinking, not just your answer.** The debugging exercise gave me trouble, but staying vocal about my reasoning mattered more than speed. Interviewers aren't just evaluating what you know — they're watching how you think under pressure.

3. **Know your failures as well as your wins.** Every project has a story about what went wrong. Knowing yours, and what you'd do differently, is what separates someone who built something from someone who just ran it.

4. **The gap between interview trivia and production engineering is real.** I blanked on textbook questions while a real pipeline was running in the background. Study the concepts, but don't let that shake your confidence in the actual work you've done.

5. **Rest is not wasted time.** The weeks I stepped away from applications and just built were the weeks I grew the most. Momentum comes from being rested and focused, not from grinding harder.

To everyone still in the search — keep building, keep showing up, and keep talking through your reasoning even when you're not sure. The right room will recognize it.
